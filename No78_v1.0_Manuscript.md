## Scope and relationship to frozen v0.1

This supplement extends the theorem of the byte-frozen v0.1 manuscript (in FROZEN_V01.zip) from k>=4 to every integer n>=k>=3. The parent contains the independent elementary star and projection lemmas, the count and density recurrences, and the analytic k>=4 proof. No result from No.75–77 is assumed. The new k=3 upper bound uses an exact finite transfer-matrix certificate and an analytic induction in the gaps, not enumeration of all large graphs.

Let H(n,k) be the induced graph of the n-cube on binary words with no cyclic block of k ones; let C_n^(k) denote its order and A_m^(k) the linear run-avoiding word count, with A_0=1. Edge general position means that no geodesic contains three distinct selected edges. Set B_n=2(C_n^(k)-A_(n-1)^(k)).

**Theorem (candidate).** For every n>=k>=3, gp_e(H(n,k))=B_n, and its maximum sets are exactly the unions of two distinct complete coordinate edge classes.

The lower bound and equality classification within sets using at most two directions follow from v0.1 §1. The full case k>=4 is v0.1 §§2–5, unchanged. It remains to prove k=3, including the equality case n=4.

## 1. Four-coordinate exclusion lemma for cyclic triple avoidance

Let n>=4, let four distinct marked coordinates be given in cyclic order, and let c be ANY four-bit pattern (no feasibility restriction is imposed). Let E denote the number of legal cyclic words differing from c at exactly two of the marked coordinates, and let C denote the total number of legal words. Then

    11 E >= 3 C.

Proof. Let the positive integer gaps g_1,...,g_4 be the cyclic distances between successive marked coordinates, summing to n. A rotation lets the first marked coordinate be 0. The state r=0,1,2 counts the trailing ones. With rows denoting source states and columns denoting destination states, write

    T_0 = [[1,0,0],[1,0,0],[1,0,0]],
    T_1 = [[0,1,0],[0,0,1],[0,0,0]],
    T = T_0+T_1.

For a prescribed marked bit pattern b, the number of legal completions is

    N_g(b) = tr( T_(b_1) T^(g_1-1) ... T_(b_4) T^(g_4-1) ).

Indeed each closed state walk gives a cyclic word with no triple ones. Every legal word has at least one zero, which forces state 0 immediately after it and determines all states uniquely; conversely the all-one word cannot give a closed walk. This proves the trace identity, with fixed labeled positions and no division by rotations. Thus C=sum_b N_g(b) and E=sum_(d_H(b,c)=2) N_g(b).

Direct matrix multiplication gives T^3=T^2+T+I. For any g_i>=4, the value L(g,c)=11E-3C is the sum of its three values with g_i replaced by g_i-1,g_i-2,g_i-3 and all other gaps and c unchanged. This follows by linearity in the corresponding power of T, without any positivity assumption on the coefficients in L. All new gaps remain positive, so lowering a gap also leaves a valid four-mark cyclic instance. Induction on the total gap sum reduces L>=0 to g in {1,2,3}^4 and all c in {0,1}^4. This inclusion of ALL c is essential: no assumption about preserving feasibility under gap reduction is made.

The finite certificate verify_v02.py verifies 81 gap tuples times 16 center patterns = 1,296 inequalities by exact integer matrix multiplication. It independently counts each projection histogram by directly enumerating all words of the corresponding length (4 through 12). Both methods agree on every histogram. Minimum E/C is 3/11 and there are eight base equality cases. This completes the computer-assisted induction. The lower bound 3/11 is sharp as a universal four-coordinate inequality (for example n=4, center pattern of weight one).

## 2. Upper bound for k=3, n>=5

Suppose S uses at least three directions. By the elementary star lemma proved in v0.1 §1, it is a star forest. If its largest star degree d<=3, then |S| <= 3C/4 < 19C/25 <= B_n, using the density recurrence proved in v0.1 §2.

If d>=4, choose any four arms of a star at vertex z. Let c be its projected pattern on these four coordinates. Every legal vertex x counted by E in §1 has two agreements and two disagreements with z on these coordinates. For any incident selected edge direction l, remove l from the four marked coordinates if it is present. At least one agreement i and one disagreement j remain. These are two different arms of the star, distinct from l. The projection of the incident edge onto i,j is a leaf of the two-arm star. The projection lemma of v0.1 §1 forbids that edge. Thus all E vertices are excluded from incidence with S. Since a star forest has no more edges than its incident vertices, |S| <= C-E <= 8C/11 < 19C/25 <= B_n. Both degree cases are strict. This proves the value and classification for all n>=5, k=3.

## 3. Small cases and equality

For n=3, k=3: C=7, B=6, and any S using at least three directions has |S|<=3C/4<6. For n=4, k=3: C=11, B=8. The degree bound gives |S|<= floor(4C/5)=8, irrespective of star degree for sets using at least three directions. To settle uniqueness of the coordinate-pair form even here, verify_v02.py separately builds the actual 11-vertex, 16-edge graph, computes BFS distances from every vertex, and enumerates every geodesic by moving along edges of increasing source distance. It generates 96 forbidden edge triples, tests all binomial(16,8)=12,870 eight-edge subsets and all binomial(16,9)=11,440 nine-edge subsets. Exactly six eight-edge sets survive, precisely the unions of two complete coordinate classes; no nine-edge set survives. Any larger set would have a nine-edge subset, so this also independently confirms the value 8.

This completes the stated candidate theorem within n>=k>=3. No n<k convention is asserted. No k=2 result is assumed or claimed by this supplement.

# Exact edge general position numbers of cyclic run-avoiding cubes for k >= 4

No.78 v0.1 — PRIVATE HOLD — 2026-11-19 JST
Author-side analytic proof candidate. Independent AI audit and Windows reproduction pending. NOVELTY INCONCLUSIVE / REFERENCES PARTIAL. Not peer reviewed. All rights reserved.

## Model and results
For integers n>=k>=3, let H(n,k) be the induced subgraph of the binary n-cube on words containing no cyclic block of k ones. Write C_n=C_n^(k) for its order, and A_m=A_m^(k) for the number of linear length-m words avoiding k consecutive ones (A_0=1). A selected edge set S is in edge general position if no three distinct selected edges lie on a common geodesic. Let B_n=2(C_n-A_(n-1)).

Theorem: For all n>=k>=4, gp_e(H(n,k))=B_n. The maximum sets are exactly the unions of any two distinct complete coordinate edge classes.

Partial result for k=3: gp_e=B_n at n=3,4. For n>=5, any S larger than B_n must use at least three coordinates and have largest star degree between 4 and 11 inclusive. No claim of equality for all k=3 is made.

No.75–77 main theorems are not assumed. The elementary projection and star lemmas used in No.77 are reproduced below. The proof of the theorem is analytic; finite checks are corroboration only.

## 1. Geometry and lower bound
The legal family is downward closed. A path via x AND y between any legal x,y attains Hamming distance, so H(n,k) is isometric in Q_n. A geodesic never repeats a coordinate. Each coordinate class has C_n-A_(n-1) edges: a legal word with a one there corresponds to its downward edge, and words with a zero there are exactly linear legal words of length n-1. Rotation gives the same count in every direction. Any union of two classes is in edge general position, of size B_n. A set using at most two coordinates cannot exceed B_n; equality requires two complete classes.

Projection lemma: If S contains two edges of a star at c, in coordinates i,j, then a selected edge in another coordinate l cannot project on i,j to a leaf of that two-arm star. Indeed let z be the corresponding leaf and w the endpoint of the edge with w_l=z_l. These vertices agree on i,j,l. Concatenate the other leaf -> c -> z, a geodesic from z to w, and the selected edge. The directions of these parts are disjoint, so this is a geodesic containing three selected edges, a contradiction.

Star lemma: If S uses at least three directions, the graph of its selected edges is a star forest. A simple three-edge path must repeat a direction, else it is a geodesic. Its directions must then be i,j,i. The two consecutive two-arm stars have leaf projections on i,j covering all four patterns, so the projection lemma forbids a third direction anywhere. Thus no simple three-edge path exists. The selected graph is bipartite, hence its nontrivial components are stars. If the maximum degree is d and E vertices are excluded from incidence with S, then |S|<=d/(d+1)(C_n-E).

## 2. Counting and density
The linear counts satisfy A_m=2^m for 0<=m<k and A_m=sum_{j=1}^k A_(m-j) for m>=k. The cyclic counts satisfy C_j=2^j-1 for 1<=j<=k under the periodic-word convention at j<k, and C_n=sum_{j=1}^k C_(n-j) for n>k. To see this, take the k-state matrix T of transitions of the run of ones: every state r=0..k-1 can move to state 0 by emitting zero, and r<k-1 can move to r+1 by emitting one. Legal cyclic words correspond bijectively to closed walks with a fixed starting index, since at least one zero determines all run states uniquely. Thus C_j=tr(T^j) for positive j. The characteristic polynomial is x^k-x^(k-1)-...-1 (companion matrix, up to transposition/convention), and Cayley-Hamilton gives the recurrence for n>k. Consequently any fixed linear combination of C_n and A_(n-1) satisfies this recurrence for n>=k+1, and a nonnegative (or positive) block of k consecutive values propagates forward.

For k>=5, B_n/C_n>10/11 for n>=k. This is equivalent to 6C_n-11A_(n-1)>0. At n=k this difference is 2^(k-1)-6>0. For n=k+t with 1<=t<=k-1, two disjoint starts of maximal runs of length at least k in a non-all-one cyclic word are impossible (would require at least 2k+2 positions). A bad non-all-one cyclic word has a unique occurrence of 0 followed by k ones. Hence C_n=2^n-1-n*2^(n-k-1). For linear length m=n-1, an illegal word similarly has exactly one maximal long-run start, either at the first position or preceded by zero. Thus A_(n-1)=2^(n-1)-(t+1)*2^(t-2). Substitution gives
6C_n-11A_(n-1)=2^(t-2)(2^(k+1)-12k-t+11)-6.
For t=1 the expression in parentheses is at least 14 (at k=5, increasing in k), hence the difference is at least 1. For 2<=t<=k-1 it is at least 2^(k+1)-13k+12>=11 (increasing for k>=5), hence the difference is >=5. The k positive bases n=k..2k-1 propagate by recurrence.

For k=4, B_n/C_n>=11/13 for n>=4: verify n=4,5,6,7 by C=(15,26,51,99), A_(n-1)=(8,15,29,56), and propagate 13B_n-11C_n. In addition B_n/C_n>6/7 for n>=6: verify n=6,7,8,9, yielding C=(51,99,191,367), A_(n-1)=(29,56,108,208), hence 7B_n-6C_n=(2,8,16,24), all positive. (At n=9: 7*318-6*367=24.) Propagate this block of four values.

For k=3, B_n/C_n>=19/25 for n>=5: the three bases n=5,6,7 have C=(21,39,71), A_(n-1)=(13,24,44), B=(16,30,54). The differences 25B-19C are (1,9,1), and the recurrence propagates them.

## 3. Uniform vertex exclusion
Suppose a star at c has d arms. Choose m pairwise nonadjacent cyclic coordinates among the arms. The induced graph of these coordinates in the cycle is a union of paths if proper, or a cycle if full. Thus at least floor(d/2) independent coordinates exist; for d odd and the coordinate set proper this improves to ceil(d/2).

Let X be a uniform legal vertex. Conditional on specified bits at any subset of the chosen coordinates, the probability of either value at any other chosen coordinate is between 1/5 and 4/5. Deleting a one injects the conditioned one class into the zero class. Turning a zero to one and clearing both cyclic neighbors gives a legal word (the new one is isolated; downward closure handles the other positions) and preserves the condition. It has at most four preimages, since only the two former neighbor bits are unknown. Thus the zero class is at most four times the one class. The same bounds apply to agreement or disagreement with the fixed center c.

Let Y be the number of disagreements on these m coordinates. The event 2<=Y<=m-2 excludes the vertex from every selected edge: after ignoring its incident edge direction, at least one agreeing and one disagreeing star coordinate remain. Their two-bit projection is a leaf of a two-arm star, forbidden by the projection lemma.

A uniform lower bound p_m on this event holds even for adaptive conditional distributions. Set f_m(s)=1 for 2<=s<=m-2 and 0 otherwise. For j=m-1,...,0 set f_j(s)=min{(4 f_(j+1)(s)+f_(j+1)(s+1))/5,(f_(j+1)(s)+4 f_(j+1)(s+1))/5}. Backward induction using the conditional probability interval proves the bound p_m=f_0(0). Exact arithmetic gives p_4=9/125, p_5=97/625, p_6=757/3125. In particular |S| <= d/(d+1)(1-p_m) C_n, and also |S| <= (1-p_m) C_n.

## 4. Upper bound for k>=5
Assume three or more selected directions. Then S is a star forest. If d<=10, |S|<=10 C_n/11<B_n. If d>=11, at least five independent coordinates exist, so |S|<=(528/625)C_n <10 C_n/11<B_n. This gives strict inequality for all sets using three or more directions.

## 5. Upper bound for k=4
Again assume three or more directions. When d<=5, |S|<=5 C_n/6<11 C_n/13<=B_n. If d=6 then n>=6, where B_n/C_n>6/7, giving strict inequality. If 7<=d<=9 and n>=8, there exist four pairwise nonadjacent arm coordinates (in particular for d=7 it is a proper subset of the cycle). Hence |S|<= (9/10)(116/125) C_n = (522/625) C_n < (11/13) C_n <= B_n. If d>=10, use five independent arms and |S|<= (528/625) C_n < (11/13) C_n <=B_n.

The sole missing case is n=d=7. All seven directions are arms of a star. Its center c is legal and has Hamming weight at most five, since six ones on a 7-cycle contain a run of six. If weight(c)>=2, the all-zero word has at least two agreements and two disagreements with c. If weight(c)<=1, select two zero positions of c and set just those two positions to one, and all other positions to zero; this word is legal for k=4 and has between two and three disagreements and at least four agreements. The projection lemma excludes the resulting vertex. Thus |S|<=7*98/8=85.75 < B_7=86. This handles all cases, including equality, and proves the theorem.

## 6. Partial result for k=3
At n=3 all sets using three directions satisfy |S|<=3*7/4<6=B_3. At n=4 the degree bound yields |S|<=floor(4*11/5)=8=B_4. At n>=5, d<=3 gives |S|<=3/4 C_n <19/25 C_n<=B_n. For d>=12 choose six independent arms, yielding |S|<=2368/3125 C_n<19/25 C_n<=B_n. The remaining d=4..11 are not resolved by these inequalities. No uniqueness claim at n=4.
